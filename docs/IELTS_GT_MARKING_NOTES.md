# General Training Writing marking notes

Source: `ielts-general-training-writing-sample-tasks-2023.pdf` (IELTS.org, 2023), converted to searchable text in `docs/ielts-general-training-writing-sample-tasks-2023.txt`.

Use these examiner examples as calibration anchors, not fixed answers. The PDF warns that there are many ways to achieve a band and its sample scripts are not definitive models.

- Task 1 is a letter. Evaluate its purpose, audience, tone, coverage of every requested bullet, greeting and closing, then organisation, vocabulary and grammar. A missing bullet or closing can lower Task Achievement even when other language is good.
- Task 2 requires a clear position or answer to every part of the question, with relevant support. Judge whether grammar and word choice errors impede communication, not just whether errors exist.
- In sample Task 2B about shopping, Script A received Band 4: it omitted why shopping is popular and did not address effects on society; spelling and grammar also strained reading.
- In the same task, Script B received Band 6 despite unclear expressions and grammar/punctuation errors. The examiner found relevant ideas, clear overall progression, and errors that generally did not reduce communication. Avoid automatically pushing an understandable response to Band 4 solely because it contains multiple local errors.
- Script C received Band 8.5: both parts were explored in depth, with clear progression and precise vocabulary; its slightly off-topic ending was a minor lapse.
- Task 1A Script A received Band 5.5 with relevant content but weaker third-bullet coverage, repetition, weak linking, and frequent grammar errors. Script B received Band 7 despite some grammar errors because its content and organisation were strong.

The PDF gives overall task bands and narrative comments, not four criterion scores. Do not infer or claim exact per-criterion scores for these samples.
